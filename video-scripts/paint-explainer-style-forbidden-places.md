# Breakdown + Script: "Places That Are Illegal to Visit (And What Happens If You Try)"

Reference video: **"The Most Guarded Places on Earth You Can't Visit"** by **The Paint Explainer**
(https://youtu.be/yMwWSX_cvrc)

---

## Part 1: Channel analysis (The Paint Explainer)

**What it is:** a channel of roughly 2M subscribers that turns facts, history and internet stories into explainers drawn in an MS Paint style. The creator stays anonymous and says they use AI for voiceover, image cleanup and research help.

**Why it works:**

| Element | What they do | Why it works |
|---|---|---|
| Visual style | Crude MS Paint stick figures and flat colours, with a few real photos dropped in | Cheap to make, easy to spot in a feed, and the rough drawings make jokes land |
| Narration | Calm, friendly, matter-of-fact voiceover (AI voice) | The flat delivery makes absurd facts funnier |
| Viewer as character | "You" are the stick figure trying to get in, getting caught, or dying | Puts the viewer into the story |
| Format | A list that gets more extreme as it goes (more guarded, more secret) | Every entry has to top the last one, which keeps retention high |
| Length | Short segments of 45–90 seconds each, 8–12 minutes in total | Each entry is like a small video with its own setup and payoff |
| Humour | Understatement, visual gags, and a callback in the final line | Keeps an "educational" video fun to watch |

**Topics in the reference video (9 places):** Area 51, the Mausoleum of Qin Shi Huang, Room 39 (North Korea), Mezhgorye (Russia), Cheyenne Mountain Complex, the Svalbard Global Seed Vault, Fort Knox, the Vatican Secret Archives, and Svalbard more generally.

**Order:** it moves from famous and slightly silly (Area 51) through mysterious (the tomb, Room 39) to "the world ends and this place survives" (Cheyenne Mountain, the Seed Vault). The money and secrets entries (Fort Knox, the Vatican Archives) go near the end.

### Hook patterns this channel uses

1. **Dare hook:** "Here are places you can't visit, and what would happen if you tried."
2. **Escalation promise:** "Each one is harder to get into than the last. The final one…"
3. **Absurd specific fact up front:** a strange number or detail in the first 5 seconds (a 22-ton door, a mercury river).
4. **"You" in danger:** the stick-figure viewer reaches the fence and lights flash on.
5. **Mini cliffhanger before each entry:** "But that's nothing compared to number 3."

### How each entry is built

1. **Name card** with a number and the place name, plus a crude map pin.
2. **What it is**, in one plain sentence.
3. **Why it's locked down**, with one surprising fact.
4. **"If you tried…"**, a gag showing the stick figure's attempt and what happens to them.
5. **A twist or bonus fact** to finish the segment.
6. **A bridge line** that teases the next entry.

## Part 2: Similar video script

**Title options:**
- *Places That Are Illegal to Visit (And What Happens If You Try)* ← recommended
- *Places on Earth Where You're Not Allowed to Exist*
- *I Tried to Visit the World's Most Forbidden Places (Not Really)*

**Thumbnail:** a Paint stick figure with a backpack at a red "NO ENTRY" sign. Behind it are an island, a vault door and a snake, with a big red arrow and the text "DON'T."

**Runtime target:** about 9–10 minutes (roughly 1,350 words of voiceover)

> ⚠️ Fact-check every number and date before recording. They come from public sources but should be checked again.

### COLD OPEN (0:00–0:30)

**[VISUAL: A Paint stick figure ("You") with a tiny backpack, standing on a spinning globe.]**

**VO:** There are places on Earth where you're not allowed to go. Not "the museum is closed today" not allowed. More like "armed guards," "venomous snakes," or "the locals will shoot arrows at your helicopter" not allowed.

**[VISUAL: The stick figure gulps. The globe stops spinning.]**

**VO:** So today we're going to try to visit all of them, one by one, each harder to get into than the last. And the final one has a perfect record of keeping people out.

**[VISUAL: Title card in rough red Paint letters: "PLACES YOU CAN'T VISIT."]**

---

### #9: CLUB 33, DISNEYLAND (0:30–1:20)

**[VISUAL: The Disney-style castle drawn badly. The stick figure is holding a churro.]**

**VO:** Let's start easy. Hidden in Disneyland's New Orleans Square is a door marked only with the number 33. Behind it is a secret private club where alcohol has been served for most of the park's history, in a park that famously sold none.

**[VISUAL: The stick figure presses its face against the door. A tiny "33" sign.]**

**VO:** Getting in isn't hard because of guards. It's hard because of money. Membership reportedly costs tens of thousands of dollars to join, plus yearly fees, and the waitlist has been so long that people waited years just for a chance.

**[VISUAL: The stick figure empties its wallet. One coin and a moth fall out.]**

**VO:** So if you tried? Nothing dramatic happens. A polite cast member smiles and says no. Which is honestly the scariest thing in Disneyland.

**VO (bridge):** Next up, a place that's locked to protect it from *you*.

---

### #8: LASCAUX CAVE, FRANCE (1:20–2:20)

**[VISUAL: A cave entrance. Inside are bad Paint copies of horses and bulls.]**

**VO:** In 1940, a group of teenagers in France followed their dog into a hole and found walls covered in paintings around 17,000 years old.

**[VISUAL: A dog falls down a hole. Four stick-figure kids follow it.]**

**VO:** The cave opened to the public, and that turned out to be the problem. Thousands of visitors breathing out carbon dioxide, warmth and moisture started growing mould and algae on the art. So in 1963 France closed it for good.

**[VISUAL: The stick figure breathes out. A green cloud spreads over a horse painting. The horse looks offended.]**

**VO:** Today only a few scientists go in, for limited time, wearing protective suits. Everyone else visits a full replica built next door.

**VO (twist):** So the only way to see the oldest art in Europe is to look at a very good copy, the same way you looked at the Mona Lisa over 200 people's heads.

**VO (bridge):** But at least Lascaux has a replica. The next place doesn't let you see the real thing *or* a copy.

---

### #7: ISE GRAND SHRINE, JAPAN (2:20–3:20)

**[VISUAL: A simple wooden Japanese shrine behind several fences.]**

**VO:** Ise Grand Shrine is the holiest site in Japanese Shinto. You can visit the grounds. But the inner sanctuary is surrounded by layers of wooden fences, and only priests and members of the imperial family may pass the last one.

**[VISUAL: The stick figure climbs one fence, then sees another, and another, then sits down.]**

**VO:** Here's the strange part. Every 20 years, the whole main shrine is torn down and rebuilt exactly, by hand, next to the old one. That has gone on for over a thousand years. The most recent rebuild was in 2013. The next is due in 2033.

**[VISUAL: Two identical shrines side by side. The stick figure looks back and forth between them, confused.]**

**VO (twist):** So it's one of the oldest sacred sites in the world, and also one of the newest buildings. And you're not invited to either one.

**VO (bridge):** Next, an island so off-limits that even a vegetable got kicked out.

---

### #6: SURTSEY, ICELAND (3:20–4:20)

**[VISUAL: The ocean erupts and a black island pops up like toast.]**

**VO:** In 1963, a volcano erupted off the coast of Iceland and created a brand new island called Surtsey. Scientists saw a rare chance to watch life arrive on completely empty land.

**[VISUAL: One bird lands. Then a seed. A scientist with a clipboard takes notes.]**

**VO:** So almost nobody is allowed to go. Only a small number of researchers can visit, and they must not bring anything that could introduce new life: no seeds, no food scraps, nothing.

**VO:** Which makes this next part awkward. A plant once sprouted on the island that had no business being there: a tomato plant. Researchers traced it to a visitor who had relieved himself outdoors. It was promptly pulled out.

**[VISUAL: A lone tomato plant. A scientist pulls it out while looking very disappointed in someone off-screen.]**

**VO (twist):** So on Surtsey, even going to the toilet can mess up a scientific study.

**VO (bridge):** On that island the danger was *you*. On the next one, the danger is everywhere.

---

### #5: SNAKE ISLAND, BRAZIL (4:20–5:20)

**[VISUAL: A green island. The longer you look, the more snakes appear in the trees.]**

**VO:** About 30 km off the coast of Brazil is Ilha da Queimada Grande, better known as Snake Island. It's the only home of the golden lancehead, one of the most venomous snakes in the Americas.

**VO:** With no predators and no humans, the snakes have the island to themselves, with estimates running into the thousands. The Brazilian Navy bans the public from landing, partly to protect people and partly to protect the snakes from smugglers.

**[VISUAL: The stick figure's rowboat gets near. A dozen snakes turn their heads at once. The boat reverses.]**

**VO (twist):** The only people who regularly visited were lighthouse keepers. Local legend has some grim stories about how that went. Today the lighthouse runs automatically.

**VO (bridge):** Snakes are scary. But they don't have 3-foot-long keys.

---

### #4: BANK OF ENGLAND GOLD VAULTS, LONDON (5:20–6:20)

**[VISUAL: An ordinary London street. Underneath, a huge cartoon vault full of gold bars.]**

**VO:** Below the streets of central London sit some of the largest gold vaults in the world, with around 400,000 bars inside. Much of it belongs to other countries.

**[VISUAL: Gold bars with tiny flags on them.]**

**VO:** The vault doors open with keys about three feet long, and getting past them involves several layers of security that the bank doesn't talk about.

**[VISUAL: The stick figure holds a key bigger than itself and falls over.]**

**VO:** If you tried to sneak in, you'd face cameras, guards and procedures you can't see coming. And even if you made it, one bar weighs around 12 kg. Good luck running.

**[VISUAL: The stick figure waddles with one bar, sweating, moving at 0.2 km/h.]**

**VO (bridge):** Gold is valuable. But what if a place guarded information instead?

---

### #3: PINE GAP, AUSTRALIA (6:20–7:20)

**[VISUAL: The Australian outback with giant white golf-ball domes in the middle of nowhere.]**

**VO:** In the middle of the Australian desert, near Alice Springs, is a facility run jointly by Australia and the United States. Officially, it supports satellite and intelligence work. Unofficially… nobody who knows will tell you.

**VO:** The airspace above it is restricted. The roads are watched. People have been prosecuted just for walking onto the land around it in protest.

**[VISUAL: The stick figure takes one step past the sign. Lights, sirens and a tiny helicopter appear.]**

**VO (twist):** The best part? It's surrounded by thousands of kilometres of desert. So even if you got in, the getaway would take about a week.

**VO (bridge):** Some places guard secrets. The next one is where a government goes if everything else is gone.

---

### #2: MOUNT WEATHER, USA (7:20–8:20)

**[VISUAL: A quiet hill in Virginia with cows. Then a cutaway shows a whole underground city.]**

**VO:** Mount Weather, in Virginia, is one of the US government's emergency command centres, built to keep the country running in a catastrophe.

**VO:** It has been in use since the Cold War and has been reported to have its own power, water and supplies. For decades, details about it barely appeared in public.

**[VISUAL: The stick figure knocks on the hill. A small hatch opens, a pair of eyes stares, and it slams shut.]**

**VO (twist):** In other words, if the world ends, there's a plan. You're just not in it.

**VO (bridge):** And then there's the last place, which doesn't need fences, guards or keys.

---

### #1: NORTH SENTINEL ISLAND, INDIA (8:20–9:30)

**[VISUAL: A small, peaceful island surrounded by coral reef. Calm music.]**

**VO:** In the Bay of Bengal sits North Sentinel Island. It's home to the Sentinelese, one of the last peoples on Earth who have had almost no contact with the outside world.

**VO:** They have made it very clear they want it to stay that way. After the 2004 tsunami, when an Indian helicopter flew over to check on them, someone on the beach responded by aiming arrows at it.

**[VISUAL: A helicopter. One stick figure on the beach draws a bow. The helicopter politely leaves.]**

**VO:** India now bans anyone from going near the island. That protects visitors, and it protects the Sentinelese, who could be wiped out by diseases they have no immunity to.

**[VISUAL: The stick figure reaches for a boat, stops, and puts it back.]**

**VO (twist):** Every other place on this list is guarded by a government, a bank or a snake. This one is guarded by the people who live there, and they've kept out the entire modern world.

### OUTRO (9:30–9:50)

**[VISUAL: The stick figure is back home on the couch with a churro, scrolling a map.]**

**VO:** So we didn't get into any of them. But we learned a lot. Mostly that the Disney cast member was the nicest "no" we got all day.

**VO:** If you know a place that should be on this list, leave it in the comments. And subscribe, because we might try to sneak somewhere else next time. Probably not, though.

## Production notes

- **Voice:** calm and deadpan, about 150 words per minute. Pause about half a second before each twist line.
- **Visuals:** keep everything rough on purpose, using Paint, Krita or a tablet drawing app. Use real photos only as small cutaways.
- **Retention:** put a visual gag at least every 8–10 seconds. Show the progress counter ("#6 of 9") in a corner.
- **Music:** light, quirky background music. Cut the music completely on the #1 reveal for impact.
- **Sensitivity:** on North Sentinel, joke about the stick figure, never about the Sentinelese. The John Allen Chau case (2018) can be mentioned in one respectful line if you want it.
- **Fact check before recording:** the Club 33 fees, the Lascaux dates, the Ise rebuild years, the Surtsey tomato story, the Snake Island population estimate, the Bank of England bar count, and the details of the North Sentinel exclusion zone.
